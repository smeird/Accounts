How this illustration works

Interest is estimated monthly using the annual interest rate divided by 12, rounded to pennies. Interest is added before each monthly payment. The first payment is one month after the balance date; month-end dates are clamped to valid calendar days. Your lender may calculate interest daily or use different payment dates, so results can differ from a statement or redemption quote.

Extra monthly payments stay fixed and aim to reduce the payoff time. A one-off overpayment reduces the opening balance before interest is charged. At a planned rate change, you choose whether the normal payment is recalculated over the remaining contractual term or kept fixed. Calculated payments have an adjusted final instalment for penny rounding.

Any balance left at the contractual term end remains owed. An illustration extending beyond that date is not permission to extend your mortgage. Repayment forecasts stop after 100 years or if the balance becomes too large to illustrate usefully.

The overpayment illustration excludes fees. Renewal comparisons include only the product fee and cashback you enter. Other fees, early-repayment charges, lender overpayment limits, payment holidays, offset accounts, interest-only borrowing and future changes other than the one you enter are not included.

# Total Assets & Retirement

Open **Planning → Total Assets & Retirement** to see assets owned today beside a retirement scenario. Start by updating and saving your [pension pots](Pensions.md), [mortgage](Mortgages.md) and bank balances. Check all source dates; values are treated as today's starting balances even when recorded earlier.

The top annual stacked columns run from your current age to an editable end age (90 for a new scenario). Positive asset components are pensions, liquid wealth and home equity; mortgage debt appears below zero for context and is already deducted in equity, so do not subtract it again from the positive stack. Switch to **Money in / out** for monthly earnings, State Pension, other income and asset withdrawals above zero, with living costs, mortgage payments, pension contributions and cash saving below zero. Flows are monthly snapshots at each age, not annual totals. An unfunded gap is shown separately and never treated as income. Tap any bar or use **Inspect age** to see that year. The final horizon point contains closing assets and no further flows. Income and asset values are never stacked together. Home equity equals property value less mortgage debt, including negative equity. Recorded open-account balances are included; closed accounts are excluded. Add only assets and debts not already represented by these sources. Other assets must be liquid or realisable at their entered value; other debts immediately offset liquid wealth.

## Explore a scenario

1. Move the retirement-age and end-age sliders, or enter exact values, to redraw the timeline. The headline shows sustainable retirement income per month and per day, and the mortgage context shows repayment age and any debt at retirement. Set retirement age, forecast end age and monthly living-cost target in today's pounds, before tax. Mortgage payments are added separately. The target uses Go-Go spending when the saved pension plan enables phases.
2. Enter a current property valuation, replacement home price and all selling, buying, tax, moving and early repayment costs. Compare cash released by downsizing now. Enable downsizing to model a move at retirement. A negative release needs funding; equity alone is not spending money.
3. Try extra monthly pension contributions and cash saving from future earnings. Both remain fixed nominal payments until retirement; salary itself is never counted as wealth. Extra pension contributions are a hypothetical zero-fee pot, including whatever employer payments and relief you enter. Saved pots keep their provider fees.
4. Optionally enter current working monthly income and living costs in today’s pounds to complete the pre-retirement cash-flow picture. They maintain purchasing power until retirement. These inputs do not invest salary surplus automatically or constrain assumed saving; review affordability when outgoings exceed earnings. Enter other retirement income and its starting age if relevant. This is an inflation-linked before-tax income stream to the end age, starting no earlier than retirement; exclude the State Pension already supplied in Pensions.
5. Compare lower, central and higher growth illustrations, the first tested whole-year retirement age meeting the target, and the yearly assets table. A shortfall can occur while inaccessible pensions or home wealth remain.
6. Select **Save scenario** to preserve assumptions. Planner defaults resets the preview only; save explicitly to keep it. Concurrent saves and changed source-plan revisions require reloading. No source planner is edited.

## Calculation boundaries

Monthly calculations honour saved pension growth, fees, inflation, access age, spending phases and user-supplied State Pension. Spending occurs before monthly growth; contributions arrive at month end and stop at retirement. Liquid savings fund withdrawals first, followed by accessible pension pots. Income first covers living costs and mortgage payments; any surplus is available but not reinvested. Negative liquid figures represent cumulative unfunded gaps, not available borrowing. Income uses the selected tax basis and is not an annuity quotation. With no target, the timeline uses central sustainable spending rather than leaving pots untouched; with a target, it shows the assets needed to fund that target. Daily income is monthly income × 12 ÷ 365.2425.

Mortgage regular payments and any saved rate reset apply from the starting balance. Saved extra payments and lump sums are excluded to avoid assuming they can be paid from the same cash included as assets. Pre-retirement payments are assumed affordable from earnings in addition to contributions and savings. Retirement withdrawals also fund mortgage payments. Any outstanding contractual-maturity debt is repaid in full, drawing on cash and accessible pension assets; insufficient funding is reported rather than assuming refinancing. Early repayment charges must be entered separately in moving costs.

A move at retirement repays outstanding mortgage debt, buys the replacement home and pays costs; only the residual becomes liquid wealth. Both property prices follow the entered nominal home growth. Moving costs increase with inflation. No equity release loan is modelled. No inheritance reserve is retained in spendable assets. All pension pots are assumed accessible at the saved common access age.

Lower/higher cases change pension and liquid-asset growth by two percentage points either side of the entered rates. They do not change home growth. These smooth-growth cases do not measure likelihood or cover contribution limits, benefits or care costs. Estimated tax and specific adverse paths are available separately. An illustrative retirement age is never a guarantee. Review [MoneyHelper's downsizing guidance](https://www.moneyhelper.org.uk/en/pensions-and-retirement/taking-your-pension/downsizing-in-retirement) and use personal provider forecasts.

## Saving and backups

One installation-wide scenario is stored with optimistic revisions in the `total_assets_plan` setting; no schema migration is required. Include **Settings**, **Pensions** and **Mortgages** in [Backup & Restore](Setup.md#backups-and-restores) to preserve the full planning context. Drafts stay in memory and never enter browser storage or service-worker caches. Tables and calculations remain available if charts cannot load.

Verification: `php tests/total_assets_test.php`, `php tests/run_tests.php`, and `tests/total_assets_browser.cjs` with Playwright and a local PHP server. Browser tests use isolated source fixtures and real PHP forecasts; they do not modify application financial data.

## Tax-aware spending, stress tests and retirement comparisons

Enable **Include estimated retirement income tax** and select England/Wales/Northern Ireland or Scotland. Sustainable monthly/daily spending and the spending target then mean after estimated tax; mortgage payments remain additional. Existing scenarios keep their before-tax basis until explicitly changed and saved. New scenarios enable tax by default. The money-in/out chart includes estimated retirement income tax as an outgoing.

The estimate annualises each month's taxable income using 2026/27 rates verified on 1 November 2026: [UK rates](https://www.gov.uk/income-tax-rates) and [Scottish rates](https://www.gov.uk/scottish-income-tax). The personal allowance taper is included. Rates and bands are held constant in today's purchasing power, assuming inflation-linked thresholds rather than predicting policy. All pension draws, State Pension and other retirement income are treated as fully taxable; no unused tax-free entitlement is assumed. Capital draws are untaxed; enter liquid growth after relevant taxes. This is one taxpayer, with no PAYE, part-year reconciliation, NI, savings/dividend taxes, CGT, reliefs, couples' allowances or tax-free lump-sum optimisation. Working earnings remain gross context only. Saved pension-planner calculations remain unchanged.

**Compare retirement ages** evaluates 60, 65, 67, 70 and the chosen age, omitting past ages and ages at/after the horizon. Each row uses identical source data, tax basis, end age and target; it shows monthly/daily sustainable spending, mortgage debt, target outcome and first shortfall. Explore a row to preview that age, then save explicitly if wanted.

**Stress-test this retirement** compares a 25% pension-value loss immediately before the first retirement withdrawal; nominal pension returns capped at 0% before fees for the first ten retirement years; and inflation two percentage points above the saved assumption throughout. Each funds the same target, or central sustainable spending when the target is zero. Results show sustainable spending and any first funding shortfall. Cash/property are not directly shocked. These are deterministic sensitivity tests, not historical replays, Monte Carlo probabilities or guarantees.

Additional validation: `php tests/retirement_tax_test.php` checks band boundaries, taper, Scottish starter/basic rates, after-tax gross-up, adverse scenarios, and comparison consistency.
